Hands-on Live Audio Experience

My live-audio experience comes from working real events in very different environments. I’ve handled unscripted open-mic situations, supported theater and production audio, and worked sound in organized school and community settings.

Open Mic & Unscripted Events

Open mic keeps you on your toes because not everything is planned. Performers may handle microphones differently, unexpected pops or feedback can happen, and problems need to be caught before they interrupt the room.

That means watching microphones and levels, muting when needed, correcting feedback quickly, and helping direct people on stage when the audio setup needs a little guidance.

Theater & Production Audio

Theater audio is a different discipline: preparation comes first. My experience includes microphone setup, cable organization, labeling, checking that equipment is powered and ready, and waiting for the cue just like the rest of the production team.

Those two environments taught me both sides of event audio: how to prepare carefully when everything is planned, and how to react quickly when it isn’t.
