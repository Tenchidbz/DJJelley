DJ Services — Small Events, Built for the Room

DJ-Jelley Entertainment is designed primarily for small parties and events of roughly 25–100 guests. That range is a practical guideline based on the room, crowd energy, and what the sound system needs to do — not just a number on a speaker specification sheet.

  • Private parties & small events
  • Fundraisers & community events
  • Crowd-responsive music
  • Music videos, animations & event visuals
  • Live sound & practical event support

My independent setup

My standard setup uses two Fender Fighter 12 powered speakers, LED lighting, and a TV for music videos, animations, and event-specific visuals. I also have additional speakers available when a room needs more coverage.

The TV normally faces the guests while I work from a smaller monitor behind the DJ table, giving the event another visual element without taking over the room.

Visuals are part of the experience

I can use music videos, animated backgrounds, personalized graphics, and other event-specific content to make the setup feel more connected to the people in the room.

A projector may be possible when the venue supports it, but lighting conditions matter. The TV is the dependable visual option in my regular setup.

There’s a difference between hearing the music and feeling the party. As a crowd grows, conversation, cheering, dancing, and room noise begin competing with the system. Larger productions with dedicated subwoofers and more headroom can deliver the extra depth and physical impact a big dance floor needs.
Planning for 100+ guests? Call me anyway. Bigger events can be handled in different ways depending on the venue, crowd energy, sound coverage, bass, lighting, and the kind of experience you want. Tell me what you’re trying to do and I can help you think through the options. If the event needs more than my independent setup is designed to provide, I can still help explain what kind of larger-scale sound, lighting, equipment, or crew support may make sense. As an independent DJ, I know what I can professionally handle. As a professional, I also know when an event needs to scale up.

Hands-on Live Audio Experience

My live-audio experience comes from working real events in very different environments. I’ve handled unscripted open-mic situations, supported theater and production audio, and worked sound in organized school and community settings.

Open Mic & Unscripted Events

Open mic keeps you on your toes because not everything is planned. Performers may handle microphones differently, unexpected pops or feedback can happen, and problems need to be caught before they interrupt the room.

That means watching microphones and levels, muting when needed, correcting feedback quickly, and helping direct people on stage when the audio setup needs a little guidance.

Theater & Production Audio

Theater audio is a different discipline: preparation comes first. My experience includes microphone setup, cable organization, labeling, checking that equipment is powered and ready, and waiting for the cue just like the rest of the production team.

Those two environments taught me both sides of event audio: how to prepare carefully when everything is planned, and how to react quickly when it isn’t.
